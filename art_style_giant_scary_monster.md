# Image Generation Specification: "Giant Scary Monster" (GSM)

This document outlines the precise prompt elements and stylistic constraints required to generate the Phase 2 "Giant Scary Monster" (GSM) from *Hide in Sheep Clothing*.

## 1. Core Concept & Perspective

* **Perspective:** Strictly 2D top-down view (bird's-eye view, 90 degrees looking straight down).

* **Subject:** A horrifying biological anomaly where a regular sheep body is the central anchor for a massive, terrifying arachnid-like creature. The central sheep body must appear hoisted HIGH UP INTO THE AIR by its monstrous appendages.

## 2. Subject Anatomy & Composition

* **Central Body:** A realistic, anatomically correct sheep body viewed directly from above. The wool is visible, illuminated against the dark ground, but visibly ruptured and torn where the monstrous limbs emerge.

* **Appendages (Giant Spider Legs):**

  * **Count & Prompt Enforcement (CRITICAL):** Exactly 20 massive SPIDER LEGS (10 per side). This count strictly refers to the monstrous spider appendages and does not include the sheep's original four little legs (which may be hidden beneath it from this angle). Because image generators strongly default to 8 or 10 total legs for arachnids, prompts **must** use visual density and comparative multipliers rather than relying on numbers alone: explicitly describe a *dense, tightly packed fan or centipede-like array of 20 distinct limbs*, emphasizing *twice as many legs as a 10-legged spider* and *a crowded, overlapping multitude of 10 limbs per flank*.

  * **Arrangement & Connection:** 10 spider legs sprouting in a continuous, tightly spaced row along the entire left flank of the sheep body (from front shoulder to rear hip), and 10 spider legs sprouting in a matching dense row along the entire right flank. The leg bases must be closely packed and overlapping as they violently burst directly *out of* the sheep's torn flesh and wool from the inside.

  * **Texture, Appearance & Articulation:** The legs are entirely hairless, smooth, and glossy black, resembling enormous spider or crustacean limbs. Critically, these legs must feature **DOUBLE** the number of joints of a regular spider leg, creating a highly segmented, unnaturally articulated, and jagged appearance. **The spider legs must have extremely SHARP and POINTY endings, almost STABBING into the ground. They should look as though the creature could EASILY PIERCE into its victim using just its giant, sharp, pointy legs.**

  * **Scale & Elevation (CRITICAL):** The spider legs are EVEN BIGGER and more colossal than a standard giant spider, completely dwarfing the central sheep body. They must VISIBLY LIFT the sheep body HIGH INTO THE AIR. Through the use of shading, forced perspective, and overlapping forms, it *must* be immediately apparent that the sheep body is towering high above the ground strictly because these spider legs are so unimaginably huge.

## 3. Art Style & Medium (Atmospheric Realism)

* **Medium:** Must look exactly like a traditional oil painting on a physical canvas.

* **Brushwork:** Visible, textured brushstrokes. Use thick impasto techniques for the illuminated wool of the sheep, the torn flesh, and the highlights on the glossy black legs.

* **Aesthetic:** Grim, macabre, and grounded in survival horror. No digital smoothing, no cartoon outlines, and no 3D render aesthetics.

## 4. Lighting, Shadow & Environment

* **Chiaroscuro & Tenebrism:** Extreme contrast between light and dark.

* **Lighting Source:** Harsh, directional, artificial lighting focused on the center (the elevated sheep body, the torn flesh where the limbs erupt, and the upper joints of the towering legs).

* **Shadows & Depth:** The edges of the canvas and the gaps between the massive legs must melt softly into deep, heavy, pitch-black shadows. The cast shadows beneath the creature must heavily emphasize the extreme vertical distance between the suspended sheep body and the ground below.

* **Environment:** The ground beneath should barely be visible, sitting far below the creature, suggested only by out-of-focus, muted, earthy brushstrokes of dirt and dead grass fading into the dark.

## 5. Color Palette

* **Base:** Muted, low-saturation, earthy tones (raw umber, muddy brown, charcoal black).

* **Highlights:** Pale, sickly yellows and harsh whites where the light strikes the sheep's back, the jagged joints of the black legs, and the exposed flesh.

## 6. Negative Prompting (What to strictly avoid)

* **NO standard arachnid leg counts:** Strictly NO 8-legged or 10-legged (5 per side) spiders. Avoid wide, sparse spacing between leg bases along the flanks; enforce a crowded, 20-legged (10 per side) silhouette.

* NO hair or fur on the spider legs (must be entirely smooth).

* NO side-angles, isometric, or 3D perspectives (must remain flat top-down, using shadow/depth to show height instead of tilting the camera).

* NO digital airbrushing, cell-shading, or vector graphics.

* NO vibrant, neon, or cheerful colors.
